Scaling Sparse Retrieval with Lightweight Scoring Heads
Diaz · Kwon · Marchetti
3 Method
We extend prior retrieval baselines with a lightweight scoring head applied to the base retriever's ranked output. The head is a two-layer MLP over pooled chunk and query embeddings, adding negligible inference cost relative to the retrieval pass itself.
Formally, given candidate set C and query q, we re-rank by s(c,q) = MLP([e_c ; e_q ; e_c ⊙ e_q]), trained with a contrastive objective on gold spans.
Unlike tree-structured approaches, our head operates on flat candidate lists and requires no index rebuild.
we observe a 4.1 point gain on the held-out split, with the largest improvements on documents exceeding 8k tokens
Figure 2 — recall@10 by document length
4 Experiments
We evaluate on HotpotQA and NarrativeQA against dense, sparse, and tree-structured baselines, reporting recall@10 and end-to-end latency.

Why the scoring head works Linked to Scaling Sparse Retrieval
The scoring head re-ranks retrieved chunks using a small learned function on top of the base retriever's output — cheap enough to run per-query but expressive enough to fix ordering mistakes.
"we observe a 4.1 point gain on the held-out split" — worth checking whether this holds on our internal eval set.
Follow-up: swap in our own scoring head architecture and re-run the long-context ablation.
