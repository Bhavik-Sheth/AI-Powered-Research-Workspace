A voice-first, AI-powered workspace for **solo researchers**.

The goal is **not to generate research or write papers**, but to eliminate research friction. The researcher remains in control of all thinking and writing, while AI handles discovery, retrieval, organisation, navigation, experiment logging(planned, remaining, in progress, done) and context management.

Everything related to a research project lives in one persistent workspace.

---

# Core Principles

- AI assists, never replaces the researcher.
- Researcher writes all content.
- Evidence over generated text.
- Persistent memory across the entire project.
- Voice-first interaction. (a local s2s model)
- One workspace for the complete research lifecycle.

---
# Features
---
## Project Workspace

Each research project acts as a self-contained workspace containing:

- Papers
- Notes
- Ideas
- Tasks
- Experiments
- References
- Voice conversations
- Knowledge graph

Everything is automatically linked together and searchable.

---

## AI Research Search

A research-first search engine that searches across academic and technical sources instead of the general web.

Sources include:
- arXiv
- Semantic Scholar
- OpenAlex
- Crossref
- Papers with Code
- GitHub
- Technical blogs

Supports natural language and voice queries.

Instead of summaries, every result is presented in a structured format:

- Problem
- Proposed method
- Datasets
- Results
- Limitations
- Code availability
- Why it is relevant

---

## Reading Workspace

An interactive paper reader where AI acts as a reading companion.

While reading a paper, the user can:

- Ask questions about any highlighted text
- Explain equations and figures
- Compare methods with previously read papers
- Jump to referenced papers
- Open implementation repositories
- View cited datasets
- Mark the paper as Relevant, somewhat relevant , not very relevant.

The goal is to understand papers without leaving the workspace.

---

## Research Memory

Persistent memory for the entire research project.

The system remembers:

- Papers read
- Notes
- Previous discussions
- Research ideas
- Important concepts
- Experiment history

Example:

> "Didn't we already read a similar method?"

The system retrieves the relevant papers, notes, and discussions.

---

## Knowledge Graph

Automatically builds connections between:

- Papers
- Methods
- Models
- Datasets
- Authors
- Research ideas
- Personal notes

Allows visual exploration of relationships and research directions.

---

## Literature Matrix

Automatically creates a comparison table for selected papers.

Typical comparison columns include:

- Problem
- Method
- Dataset
- Results
- Strengths
- Limitations
- Personal notes

The table remains fully editable by the researcher.

---

## Research Feed

A personalized feed of newly published work based on the current research project.

Tracks:

- New papers
- New code repositories
- New datasets
- Technical blog posts

Highlights only content relevant to the active research topics.

---

## Writing Workspace

A distraction-free LaTeX editor integrated with the research workspace.

The workspace provides:

- Citation search and insertion
- Reference management
- Figure and table management
- Cross-reference support
- Consistency checks
- Missing citation detection

The AI never writes sections of the paper automatically. It assists with verification and organization while the researcher remains the author.

---

## Voice Companion

Voice is available throughout the workspace.

Examples:

> "Find papers newer than this."

> "Compare this method with RAPTOR."

> "Open the implementation."

> "Show papers using this dataset."

> "Summarize my notes from yesterday."

Voice acts as a natural interface for navigating the workspace instead of replacing it.

---

# Vision

Research Companion OS is a persistent workspace that combines research search, paper reading, note-taking, writing, memory, and project organization into a single system.

Instead of replacing researchers, it helps them spend less time searching and organizing information, and more time thinking and conducting research.
